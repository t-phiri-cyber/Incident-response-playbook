\# Post-Incident Review – DATA-004



\## 1. Incident Information

\- Incident reference: DATA-004

\- Incident title: Unauthorised External Disclosure of Customer Data

\- Incident category: Personal Data Breach

\- Date of incident: 3 November 2026 – Simulation

\- Date of review: 3 November 2026 – Simulation

\- Incident lead: Northstar Security Incident Lead

\- Review participants: IT Team, Incident Response Team, Privacy Team

\- Final severity: SEV 2 – High



\## 2. Executive Summary

An employee accidentally emailed a spreadsheet containing 250 customer records to an unauthorised external recipient.



The recipient opened and downloaded the attachment. The spreadsheet contained customer names, email addresses and dates of birth.



The organisation contacted the recipient, requested deletion, preserved evidence, assessed the impact and completed the simulated ICO and customer notification process.



The incident was closed following formal approval in the exercise.



\## 3. Root Cause Analysis

\- Primary root cause: Accidental external transmission of customer information.

\- Contributing factors: Insufficient controls and warnings for external email attachments.

\- Security controls that failed: No effective additional approval or DLP control prevented the disclosure.

\- Existing controls that worked: Incident reporting, email investigation and privacy escalation.

\- Preventability: Additional technical and procedural controls could have reduced the likelihood of the incident.

\- Supporting evidence: Original email, delivery records and recipient confirmation (all simulated).



\## 4. Detection and Response Evaluation

\- Incident detected: Reported by the employee to IT.

\- Escalation: Incident lead and privacy team notified.

\- Severity classification: Appropriate SEV 2 classification.

\- Containment: Recipient contacted and deletion requested.

\- Evidence preservation: Simulated email and communication records documented.

\- Communication: Recipient and affected customers contacted in the scenario.

\- Recovery: Preventative improvements identified and assigned.



\## 5. Impact Assessment

\- Affected systems: Corporate email.

\- Affected accounts: Sending employee's email account.

\- Affected individuals: 250 customers.

\- Data affected: Names, email addresses and dates of birth.

\- Operational impact: Incident investigation and response required.

\- Financial impact: Not quantified.

\- Reputational impact: Potential customer confidence impact.

\- Regulatory implications: ICO notification and customer communication required in the scenario.



\## 6. What Went Well?

\- Employee reported the incident.

\- IT investigated delivery records promptly.

\- Recipient was contacted.

\- Original evidence was preserved in the scenario.

\- Privacy team completed a risk assessment.

\- Affected customers were informed.

\- Preventative improvements were identified.



\## 7. What Went Wrong?

\- Customer information was sent externally without adequate safeguards.

\- The spreadsheet was not encrypted.

\- The recipient downloaded and opened the attachment.

\- Email recall was unsuccessful.

\- Independent verification of deletion was unavailable.



\## 8. Lessons Learned

\- Introduce stronger controls for external transmission of personal data.

\- Review email permissions and external sharing restrictions.

\- Consider DLP monitoring and approval requirements.

\- Improve employee awareness of personal data handling.

\- Apply appropriate encryption to sensitive information.

\- Maintain clear incident reporting and escalation procedures.
