\# Data Breach Incident Response Playbook



\## 1. Purpose

This playbook establishes procedures for identifying, assessing, containing, investigating and responding to suspected or confirmed data breaches.



\## 2. Scope

Applies to suspected or confirmed unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration, loss or destruction of organisational information.



Covers:

\- Personal data

\- Employee records

\- Customer information

\- Financial information

\- Commercially confidential information

\- Intellectual property



\## 3. Indicators of a Data Breach

\- Unauthorised access to confidential files.

\- Personal information sent to an incorrect recipient.

\- Unusual file downloads or transfers.

\- Missing or stolen devices containing organisational data.

\- Unauthorised changes to records.

\- Suspicious access to cloud storage or shared folders.

\- External reports of exposed organisational information.

\- Unusual database or email activity.



\## 4. Initial Assessment

1\. Record when and how the suspected breach was discovered.

2\. Identify the systems, information and individuals potentially affected.

3\. Establish whether the incident is suspected or confirmed.

4\. Determine whether unauthorised access or disclosure occurred.

5\. Identify the information classification and sensitivity.

6\. Assign an initial severity level.

7\. Notify the incident lead and privacy team where appropriate.



\## 5. Containment

1\. Restrict unauthorised access using approved security controls.

2\. Revoke exposed sessions, tokens and credentials where necessary.

3\. Correct incorrect sharing permissions.

4\. Restrict compromised accounts and affected systems.

5\. Prevent further unauthorised disclosure.

6\. Preserve evidence before making changes.

7\. Coordinate containment with relevant system owners.



\## 6. Evidence Preservation

Collect and securely preserve:

\- Relevant system and application logs.

\- Access and authentication records.

\- File access, download and modification history.

\- Email records and message headers.

\- Data transfer records and timestamps.

\- User and administrator activity.

\- Copies of relevant communications.

\- Records of containment actions and decisions.



Maintain an evidence register documenting the source, collection time, collector, integrity and storage location.



\## 7. Investigation and Impact Assessment

1\. Establish the root cause and entry point.

2\. Determine what information was accessed, disclosed, changed or lost.

3\. Identify affected individuals and organisations.

4\. Establish whether data was copied, downloaded or transferred.

5\. Determine the number and categories of records involved.

6\. Assess potential consequences for affected individuals.

7\. Identify any continuing access or persistence.

8\. Document confirmed facts separately from assumptions.



\## 8. UK GDPR and Regulatory Assessment

1\. Refer suspected personal data breaches to the Data Protection Officer or privacy lead.

2\. Determine whether a personal data breach has occurred.

3\. Assess the likelihood and severity of risk to individuals' rights and freedoms.

4\. Determine whether ICO notification is required.

5\. Where notification is required, notify the ICO without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of awareness.

6\. Assess whether affected individuals must be informed because the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms.

7\. Document the assessment and reasons for reporting or not reporting.

8\. Maintain records of the breach and actions taken.



\## 9. Recovery

1\. Restore authorised access and correct security configurations.

2\. Recover affected information from trusted backups where required.

3\. Verify that unauthorised access has been removed.

4\. Reset exposed credentials and revoke compromised sessions.

5\. Validate system integrity and access controls.

6\. Monitor for further suspicious activity.

7\. Obtain approval before returning affected services to normal operation.



\## 10. Escalation Criteria

Escalate incidents where there is:

\- Large-scale exposure of personal information.

\- Exposure of financial, payroll or sensitive information.

\- Confirmed data exfiltration.

\- Compromise of privileged accounts or critical systems.

\- Significant business disruption.

\- A likely high risk to affected individuals.

\- Ongoing unauthorised disclosure.



\## 11. Incident Closure

Close the incident only when:

\- The breach has been contained.

\- The scope and impact have been assessed.

\- Evidence has been preserved.

\- Required reporting and communications have been addressed.

\- Recovery and security validation are complete.

\- The incident report and evidence register are updated.

\- The incident lead has approved closure.



\## 12. Post-Incident Review

Review:

\- Root cause and control failures.

\- Effectiveness of detection and containment.

\- Impact on individuals and the organisation.

\- Regulatory and communication decisions.

\- Preventative improvements and assigned actions.
